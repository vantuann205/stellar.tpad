01Problem

Launching a token means bootstrapping a market you cannot afford

Minting a token is trivial. Making it tradeable is not — and that gap is where most launches die.

Liquidity has to be bought

A new token needs a paired pool before anyone can trade it. The creator must supply both sides — capital most early projects simply do not have.

That pool is the rug

Whoever funds the LP can withdraw it. The single largest category of loss in token launches is the creator removing the liquidity they provided.

No price until someone sets one

Before a pool exists there is no price discovery at all. The first buyer is guessing, and the creator has every incentive to let them.

The result is an ecosystem where launching is easy, trading is fragile, and buyers price in a rug that frequently arrives. Every honest creator pays that risk premium too.

02Solution

Replace the pool with a curve

Stellar TPad prices tokens from a bonding curve held inside a Soroban contract. There is no LP to fund, and none to withdraw.

price(supply) = base_price + slope × (sold_supply / 10,000,000)

buy  →  contract mints against the curve, takes XLM, charges 0.5%
sell →  contract burns against the curve, returns XLM, charges 0.5%

Liquidity from block one

The curve quotes a bid and an ask the moment a token exists. No seeding round, no waiting for a counterparty.

Nothing to rug

Reserves live in the bonding-curve contract and are only reachable through buy and sell. There is no withdrawal function for a creator to call.

Exit is always open

Selling back into the curve is a contract call, not a search for a buyer. A holder is never waiting for someone else to take the other side.

03Why now

Bonding curves proved the demand. Stellar makes the economics work.

What changedWhy it matters here
Curve launchpads found product-market fitThe model is validated on other chains. The open question is no longer whether users want it.
Soroban reached MainnetStellar can now run the contract logic a curve needs — cross-contract deployment, persistent storage, typed events.
Stellar fees are a rounding errorA launchpad is a high-frequency, low-ticket product. On a chain where a trade costs a fraction of a cent, small trades stay economic.
Five-second finalityCurve pricing moves with every trade. Slow settlement means stale quotes and failed transactions.
The gap we are filling. Stellar has the settlement layer and the user base, but no native curve-based launchpad. We are early on a chain that is late to this category — which is the good version of that trade.

        │
        ├── buy  →  XLM in,  tokens out,  0.5% → treasury
        └── sell →  tokens in, XLM out,   0.5% → treasury

For creators

No capital to raise, no pool to seed, no listing to negotiate. Name the token and it is live with a market.

For traders

A quoted price before the first trade, a guaranteed counterparty, and reserves that no key can drain.

Standards-compliant

Tokens are SEP-41 — name, symbol, decimals, balance, transfer, burn. They work in any Stellar wallet, not only in ours.

Supply is fixed at launch

Minting is closed after the initial supply. A creator cannot inflate the token out from under its holders.

07Business model

0.5% of every trade, in both directions

The fee is charged by the contract, not invoiced by us. It is taken on the way in and on the way out, and routed to a treasury address on every single trade.

buy   cost   = curve_cost;    fee = cost × 0.5%;      user pays cost + fee
sell  proceeds = curve_proceeds; fee = proceeds × 0.5%;  user receives proceeds − fee

fee → treasury, atomically, inside the same contract call

Volume-linked, not user-linked

Revenue scales with trading rather than with headcount. One active token can out-earn a hundred dormant ones.

Symmetric

Charging both sides means revenue does not depend on a market going up. Volatility is the input, not direction.

Non-custodial

We never hold user funds. The fee is a transfer inside a contract call the user authorised.

08Market opportunity

A proven category, arriving on a chain that does not have it

TAM — curve-based launchpads

The category is established across major L1s and is measured in billions of cumulative trading volume. Stellar TPad competes for the share of that behaviour that follows low fees and fast finality.

SAM — the Stellar ecosystem

Stellar's existing wallet base and asset issuers, none of whom currently have a native curve-priced launch venue.

SOM — first mover on Soroban

Live contracts and real trades today. The realistic near-term target is the creators already building on Stellar who need a market without funding one.

On the numbers. This slide deliberately carries no cited market size. Launchpad volume figures move weekly and vary widely by source, and a deck that quotes an unverifiable TAM is worth less than one that does not. The bottom-up economics on the previous slide are derived from the contract's own fee constant and can be checked against the deployed code.

Where we win

Not on being the first bonding curve — on being the one where a $5 trade is still worth making. Fee-heavy chains push launchpads toward large tickets and speculative volume. Stellar's cost structure supports the small, frequent trades a curve is actually designed for.

What compounds is the token registry itself: every token launched here is permanently priced by our curve contract, and its holders trade through it for the life of the token. Being first to that registry on Stellar is the position worth holding.

10Roadmap

Depth before breadth

Shipped  Mainnet launchpad

Factory, bonding curve and SEP-41 token live. 3 tokens launched, 25 wallets, 54 verified trades, CI on every push.

Next  Reserve headroom guard

Prevent a buyer from spending into Stellar's minimum-balance reserve and stranding their own position. Highest-priority fix.

Next  Graduation to a DEX pool

Automatic migration of curve reserves into an AMM pool once a token reaches a threshold — the standard lifecycle for this category.

Later  Configurable curve parameters

Let creators choose base price and slope within audited bounds instead of a single default shape.

Later  Creator revenue share

Route part of the trading fee to the token creator, aligning them with sustained volume rather than a launch-day exit.

Later  Third-party audit

Published security material today is an internal review. An external audit gates any increase in what the contracts are trusted to hold.

Known issue, stated openly. A buyer who spends their entire XLM balance cannot currently pay the fee to sell back, because Stellar reserves 1 XLM per account. The tokens are not lost — the account needs a small top-up — but the flow should prevent it rather than explain it. It is the first item after this submission.
