01Problem

Launching a token means bootstrapping a market you cannot afford

Minting a token is trivial. Making it tradeable is not — and that gap is where most launches die.

02Solution

Replace the pool with a curve

Stellar TPad prices every token from a bonding curve held inside a Soroban contract. There is no LP to fund — and none to withdraw.

Liquidity from block one

No seeding round, no waiting for a counterparty to appear.

Nothing to rug

Reserves are reachable only through buy and sell. No withdrawal function exists.

Exit is always open

Selling back is a contract call, not a search for a buyer.

03Why now

Bonding curves proved the demand. Stellar makes the economics work.

What changedWhy it matters here
Curve launchpads found product-market fitThe model is validated on other chains. Whether users want it is no longer the open question.
Soroban reached MainnetStellar can now run what a curve needs: cross-contract deployment, persistent storage, typed events.
Stellar fees are a rounding errorA launchpad is high-frequency and low-ticket. Where a trade costs a fraction of a cent, small trades stay economic.
Five-second finalityCurve pricing moves with every trade. Slow settlement means stale quotes and failed transactions.
The gap we are filling. Stellar has the settlement layer and the user base, but no native curve-priced launch venue. We are early on a chain that is late to this category — which is the good version of that trade.

04Product

One transaction from idea to tradeable token

For creators

No capital to raise, no pool to seed, no listing to negotiate.

For traders

A quoted price before the first trade and a guaranteed counterparty.

Standards-compliant

SEP-41 tokens work in any Stellar wallet, not only in ours.

Supply fixed at launch

Minting closes after initial supply. No inflating out from under holders.

Where we win

Not on being the first bonding curve — on being the one where a $5 trade is still worth making. Fee-heavy chains push launchpads toward large tickets and speculative volume. Stellar's cost structure supports the small, frequent trades a curve is actually designed for.

09Competition

Where we sit

Defensibility, honestly assessed

The contract logic is not a moat — a bonding curve is well understood and reimplementable. What compounds is the token registry itself: every token launched here is permanently priced by our curve contract, and its holders trade through it for the life of the token. Being first to that registry on Stellar is the position worth holding.

10Roadmap

Depth before breadth

Shipped  Mainnet launchpad

Factory, bonding curve and SEP-41 token live. 3 tokens, 25 wallets, 54 verified trades, CI on every push.

Next  Reserve headroom guard

Stop a buyer spending into Stellar's minimum-balance reserve and stranding their own position. Highest priority.

Next  Graduation to a DEX pool

Migrate curve reserves into an AMM pool once a token passes a threshold — the standard lifecycle for this category.

Later  Configurable curve parameters

Let creators choose base price and slope within audited bounds instead of one default shape.

Later  Creator revenue share

Route part of the trading fee to the creator, aligning them with sustained volume rather than a launch-day exit.

Later  Third-party audit

Published security material today is an internal review. An external audit gates any increase in what the contracts hold.

Known issue, stated openly. A buyer who spends their entire XLM balance cannot currently pay the fee to sell back, because Stellar reserves 1 XLM per account. The tokens are not lost — the account needs a small top-up — but the flow should prevent this rather than explain it. First item after this submission.
